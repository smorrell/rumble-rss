## Top ten programs to consider

These are specific Ontario university programs, ranked by academic and interest fit rather than by admissions likelihood or location. Verify current program names, admission requirements, and supplementary application requirements before applying.

1. **Computing (B.CmpH), with the Computing and the Creative Arts specialization** — Queen's University. Combines computing with art, drama, film, or music; this is a specialization, not a separate B.CmpH/BFA dual degree.

Confirm with UTM whether DEM is currently an available program of study and its post-first-year entry requirements. This is near home, so it is not an away-at-school option.

5. **Interactive Media and Design (BIT)** — Carleton University, in partnership with Algonquin College. Combines technology, design, and digital media.
6. **Theatre and Performance Studies** — University of Toronto Scarborough. Fits his interests in drama and dancing; explore production and arts-management opportunities alongside performance.
7. **Business Management (BComm), with a Marketing Management major** — Toronto Metropolitan University. Students choose among specialized majors after the shared first year; connects business with marketing, creative campaigns, and audience engagement.
8. **Human Resource Management (BHRM)** — York University. A direct fit for his interest in HR and organizational strengths.
9. **Marketing (BComm)** — York University. Connects business with marketing and audience engagement.
10. **Accounting (BComm)** — York University. Builds on his math strengths and accounting interest; compare the professional pathway and typical work demands.

## Other pathways to keep in mind

Teaching remains worth exploring, but in Ontario it generally involves completing an undergraduate degree and then teacher education; it is not one single direct-entry program choice. Occupational therapy and speech-language pathology typically require further professional education, so check science prerequisites and graduate-program requirements before treating them as undergraduate options.
